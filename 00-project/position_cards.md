# PhD Position Cards

How to use: for now, just skim each card to know what each position is likely
to ask. When an interview invitation arrives, start from that card and work
through its "Prepare after the invitation" list in depth.

Supervisor and topic information comes from the job ads and your own
application letters. The supervisors' latest papers haven't been checked one by
one; check them when an invitation arrives.

**Three questions every interview will ask** (prepare these regardless of position):
1. Why did you move from design to computer science, and why a PhD?
2. Tell us about your most representative project. (InstantSim, 5-minute version)
3. Where do you see yourself in five years?

## 1. KTH: Urban Heat Prediction (Josephine Sullivan)
- **Position**: PA-2026-2917, Urban Heat Prediction Using AI and Earth Observation, Division of Robotics, Perception and Learning (RPL)
- **Topic**: fuse satellite data, weather forecasts and crowdsourced weather stations, and use a conditional diffusion model to generate probabilistic high-resolution urban temperature maps; essentially an inverse problem. Collaboration with Prof. Ban's group in urban planning and the RISE institute
- **What your letter says**: your current model is deterministic, trained on simulated data, and doesn't know where it is uncertain. This project starts from real but incomplete observations and outputs probabilities, which is the direction you want to go. You also said upfront that you haven't worked with diffusion models or remote sensing data
- **Most likely questions**:
  1. Roughly how does a diffusion model work? How is it different from the GAN you used?
  2. Observations are sparse (few weather stations). How would you train and validate?
  3. How do your sky exposure and surface temperature work relate to urban air temperature?
  4. How do you evaluate whether a probabilistic prediction is good?
  5. How strong is your math background?
- **Prepare after the invitation**: the intuition behind diffusion (illustrated articles), basic remote sensing concepts, how probabilistic predictions are evaluated. Math will come up; prepare an honest answer

## 2. Chalmers: Doctoral Students in AI (Julian Togelius's new group)

## 3. MPI / Saarland University: 3D Vision (Jan Eric Lenssen)

- **Supervisor**: 3D scene representation and generation. Your letter mentions his SceneTok (encodes a scene into a few tokens and generates new scenes in seconds) and MEt3R (measures whether generated multi-view images are 3D-consistent)
- **Your proposed direction**: predict physical properties (sun exposure, sky view, shadows) directly from learned 3D scene representations, to evaluate whether generated scenes are physically plausible and to guide generation toward physical constraints
- **Most likely questions**:

  2. How would you predict sun exposure from a 3D representation? Where does the training data come from?
  3. How do you quantify "physically plausible"?
